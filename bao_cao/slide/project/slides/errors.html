We expected ALS to lose to the category baseline for users with few interactions and for users who stay in one category. Both hypotheses were wrong: ALS wins in every group, including the three-to-four-interaction users who are two thirds of the test set, and single-category users are the easiest case for both methods. The real failure is the long tail: when the items a user touches next month are all unpopular, both methods almost always score zero. That is the same popularity bias as on the next slide, and it points to long-tail candidate sources rather than more ALS tuning.
